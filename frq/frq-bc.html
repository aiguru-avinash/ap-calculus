FRQ 1 Integration by Parts BC

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int x^2 e^x\,dx$.

Scoring Rubric (8 points)
2 ptsFirst IBP: $u=x^2$, $dv=e^x\,dx$
2 ptsSecond IBP: $u=x$, $dv=e^x\,dx$
2 ptsCorrectly combine all terms
2 ptsFinal answer with $+C$

Solution — Two Applications of IBP

First IBP: $u=x^2$, $du=2x\,dx$, $dv=e^x\,dx$, $v=e^x$.

$$\int x^2 e^x\,dx = x^2e^x - \int 2xe^x\,dx$$

Second IBP on $\int xe^x\,dx$: $u=x$, $du=dx$, $dv=e^x\,dx$, $v=e^x$.

$$\int xe^x\,dx = xe^x - e^x$$

Combine:

$$\int x^2 e^x\,dx = x^2e^x - 2(xe^x-e^x)+C = x^2e^x-2xe^x+2e^x+C$$

$$\boxed{=e^x(x^2-2x+2)+C}$$

Verify: $\dfrac{d}{dx}[e^x(x^2-2x+2)]=e^x(x^2-2x+2)+e^x(2x-2)=e^x\cdot x^2$ ✓

FRQ 2 Partial Fractions BC

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int\dfrac{3x+1}{(x+1)(x-2)}\,dx$.

Scoring Rubric (9 points)
3 ptsPartial fraction decomposition: find $A$ and $B$
3 ptsIntegrate each term
3 ptsCorrect final answer

Solution

$\dfrac{3x+1}{(x+1)(x-2)}=\dfrac{A}{x+1}+\dfrac{B}{x-2}$

$3x+1=A(x-2)+B(x+1)$

$x=2$: $7=3B\Rightarrow B=7/3$

$x=-1$: $-2=-3A\Rightarrow A=2/3$

$$\int\frac{3x+1}{(x+1)(x-2)}dx=\frac{2}{3}\int\frac{dx}{x+1}+\frac{7}{3}\int\frac{dx}{x-2}$$

$$=\boxed{\frac{2}{3}\ln|x+1|+\frac{7}{3}\ln|x-2|+C}$$

FRQ 3 Logistic Growth BC

A population grows logistically: $\dfrac{dP}{dt}=0.2P\!\left(1-\dfrac{P}{800}\right)$, $P(0)=50$.

(a) What is the carrying capacity? (b) At what population is growth fastest? (c) Find $\lim_{t\to\infty}P(t)$.

Scoring Rubric (7 points)
2 ptsPart (a): $M=800$
3 ptsPart (b): growth fastest at $P=M/2=400$, with justification
2 ptsPart (c): $\lim P(t)=800$

Solution

(a) The carrying capacity is $\boxed{M=800}$ (the population stabilizes here).

(b) Growth rate $\dfrac{dP}{dt}=f(P)=0.2P(1-P/800)$ is maximized when $\dfrac{d}{dP}[P(1-P/800)]=1-P/400=0\Rightarrow P=400=M/2$.

Maximum growth rate $=0.2(400)(1-400/800)=0.2(400)(0.5)=40$ individuals/time unit.

Growth is fastest when $\boxed{P=400}$.

(c) As $t\to\infty$: $P(t)\to800$ (the equilibrium). $\boxed{\lim_{t\to\infty}P(t)=800}$

Logistic growth curve: inflection (fastest growth) at $P=400$, asymptote at $P=800$.

FRQ 7 Geometric Series — Sum & Convergence BC

FRQ 8 Ratio Test & Interval of Convergence BC

Find the interval of convergence of $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\dfrac{(-1)^n x^n}{n\cdot 3^n}$. Check endpoints.

Scoring Rubric (10 points)
3 ptsApply Ratio Test and find $R=3$
2 ptsIdentify preliminary interval $(-3,3)$
3 ptsCheck $x=3$ and $x=-3$
2 ptsState interval of convergence correctly

Solution

Ratio Test: $\left|\dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right|=\left|\dfrac{(-1)^{n+1}x^{n+1}}{(n+1)3^{n+1}}\cdot\dfrac{n3^n}{(-1)^n x^n}\right|=\dfrac{n}{n+1}\cdot\dfrac{|x|}{3}\to\dfrac{|x|}{3}$

Converges when $\dfrac{|x|}{3}<1\Rightarrow|x|<3$. Radius $R=3$.

Check $x=3$: $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\dfrac{(-1)^n 3^n}{n\cdot3^n}=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\dfrac{(-1)^n}{n}$. Alternating Harmonic Series — converges (AST). ✓

Check $x=-3$: $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\dfrac{(-1)^n(-3)^n}{n\cdot3^n}=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\dfrac{(-1)^n(-1)^n}{n}=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\dfrac{1}{n}$. Harmonic Series — diverges. ✗

Interval of convergence: $\boxed{(-3,3]}$

FRQ 9 Taylor Polynomial BC

Find the Taylor polynomial $T_4(x)$ for $f(x)=\cos x$ centered at $a=0$. Use it to approximate $\cos(0.5)$ and bound the error.

Scoring Rubric (9 points)
3 pts$T_4(x)=1-x^2/2!+x^4/4!$
2 ptsEvaluate $T_4(0.5)$
4 ptsLagrange error bound

Solution

Derivatives of $\cos x$: $f=\cos x$, $f'=-\sin x$, $f''=-\cos x$, $f'''=\sin x$, $f^{(4)}=\cos x$.

At $x=0$: $f(0)=1$, $f'(0)=0$, $f''(0)=-1$, $f'''(0)=0$, $f^{(4)}(0)=1$.

$$T_4(x)=1+0\cdot x+\frac{-1}{2!}x^2+0\cdot x^3+\frac{1}{4!}x^4=\boxed{1-\frac{x^2}{2}+\frac{x^4}{24}}$$

$T_4(0.5)=1-\dfrac{0.25}{2}+\dfrac{0.0625}{24}=1-0.125+0.002604\approx0.877604$

Actual: $\cos(0.5)\approx0.877583$. Error $\approx0.000021$.

Lagrange Error: Next term involves $f^{(5)}(c)=-\sin c$. $|f^{(5)}(c)|\le1$ for all $c$.

$|R_4(0.5)|\le\dfrac{1\cdot(0.5)^5}{5!}=\dfrac{0.03125}{120}\approx0.000260$

So $|\cos(0.5)-T_4(0.5)|<0.00026$. (Actual error $0.000021\ll0.00026$ ✓)

FRQ 10 Maclaurin Series from Substitution BC

$\sin(x^2)=x^2-\dfrac{x^6}{6}+\dfrac{x^{10}}{120}-\cdots$

$\dfrac{\sin(x^2)}{x}=x-\dfrac{x^5}{6}+\dfrac{x^9}{120}-\cdots=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty\dfrac{(-1)^n x^{4n+1}}{(2n+1)!}$

$$=\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{(-1)^n}{(4n+2)(2n+1)!}\approx0.5-0.0278+0.00083-\cdots\approx\boxed{0.473}$$

FRQ 14 Polar Curve — Slope & Tangent BC

At $\theta=\pi$: $dx/d\theta=0$ too (both zero) — need careful analysis; this is the cusp. Horizontal tangents at $\boxed{\theta=\pi/3}$ and $\boxed{\theta=5\pi/3}$.

Part (a)

$\dfrac{dx}{dt}=2t$, $\dfrac{dy}{dt}=3t^2-3=3(t^2-1)=3(t-1)(t+1)$

Horizontal: $dy/dt=0\Rightarrow t=\pm1$. $dx/dt|_{t=1}=2\ne0$, $dx/dt|_{t=-1}=-2\ne0$. ✓

Horizontal tangents at $\boxed{t=1}$ (point $(0,-2)$) and $\boxed{t=-1}$ (point $(0,2)$).

Part (b)

$\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{3(t^2-1)}{2t}$

$\dfrac{d}{dt}\!\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)=\dfrac{6t\cdot2t-3(t^2-1)\cdot2}{4t^2}=\dfrac{12t^2-6t^2+6}{4t^2}=\dfrac{6t^2+6}{4t^2}=\dfrac{3(t^2+1)}{2t^2}$

$\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}=\dfrac{d(dy/dx)/dt}{dx/dt}=\dfrac{3(t^2+1)/(2t^2)}{2t}=\dfrac{3(t^2+1)}{4t^3}$

At $t=2$: $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}=\dfrac{3(5)}{4(8)}=\dfrac{15}{32}>0$. Curve is concave up at $t=2$.

FRQ 22 Term-by-Term Differentiation / Integration of Power Series BC

Given $f(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty\dfrac{x^{2n+1}}{2n+1}$ for $|x|<1$: (a) Show $f'(x)=\dfrac{1}{1-x^2}$. (b) What function does $f$ represent?

Scoring Rubric (7 points)
3 ptsDifferentiate term by term: $f'(x)=\sum x^{2n}$
2 ptsRecognize as geometric: sum $=\frac{1}{1-x^2}$
2 ptsIntegrate $1/(1-x^2)=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left|\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right|$ → identify $f=\tanh^{-1}(x)$

Part (a)

Differentiate term by term: $f'(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty x^{2n}=1+x^2+x^4+\cdots$

This is geometric with $r=x^2$, $|r|<1$. Sum $=\dfrac{1}{1-x^2}$. ✓

Part (b)

$\displaystyle\int\dfrac{dx}{1-x^2}=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left|\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right|=\tanh^{-1}(x)$ for $|x|<1$.

Since $f(0)=0$ and $f'(x)=1/(1-x^2)$, we have $\boxed{f(x)=\tanh^{-1}(x)=\dfrac{1}{2}\ln\dfrac{1+x}{1-x}}$, the inverse hyperbolic tangent.

FRQ 23 Polar Conversion & Intersection BC

Find all intersection points of $r=\sin\theta$ and $r=\cos\theta$ in $[0,2\pi]$. Convert each intersection to rectangular form.

Scoring Rubric (8 points)
3 ptsAlgebraic intersection + pole check
3 ptsConvert intersection to Cartesian
2 ptsIdentify the circles in rectangular form

Solution

Set equal: $\sin\theta=\cos\theta\Rightarrow\tan\theta=1\Rightarrow\theta=\pi/4,5\pi/4$.

$r=\sin(\pi/4)=\sqrt{2}/2$ (positive) ✓; $r=\sin(5\pi/4)=-\sqrt{2}/2$ (negative — same point in polar).

Also check the pole: $r=0$ when $\theta=0$ (for $\sin\theta$) and $\theta=\pi/2$ (for $\cos\theta$) — different $\theta$ values but same point $(0,0)$.

Intersection 1: $\theta=\pi/4$, $r=\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.

$x=r\cos\theta=\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}$; $y=\dfrac{1}{2}$. Point: $\boxed{\left(\dfrac{1}{2},\dfrac{1}{2}\right)}$

Intersection 2: the pole $(0,0)$.

The circles are $r=\sin\theta\Leftrightarrow x^2+(y-1/2)^2=1/4$ and $r=\cos\theta\Leftrightarrow(x-1/2)^2+y^2=1/4$.

FRQ 24 Root Test & Series Analysis BC

Use the Root Test to determine convergence: $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\left(\dfrac{2n}{3n+1}\right)^n$.

Scoring Rubric (7 points)
2 ptsApply Root Test: $\lim |a_n|^{1/n}$
3 ptsCompute limit correctly: $2/3 < 1$
2 ptsConclude convergence

Solution

$a_n=\left(\dfrac{2n}{3n+1}\right)^n$. Root Test: $L=\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}|a_n|^{1/n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{2n}{3n+1}$.

$\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{2n}{3n+1}=\dfrac{2}{3}<1$.

Since $L=\dfrac{2}{3}<1$, by the Root Test the series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\left(\dfrac{2n}{3n+1}\right)^n$ converges absolutely.

FRQ 25 Mixed BC — Comprehensive Problem BC

Let $f(x)=\arctan x$.

(a) Find the Maclaurin series for $f$ (derive it, don't just state it).

(b) Use the series to approximate $\arctan(0.5)$ with error less than $0.001$.

(c) Use $f(x)=\arctan x$ to show that $\pi=4\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty\dfrac{(-1)^n}{2n+1}$.

Scoring Rubric (12 points)
4 ptsPart (a): integrate $1/(1+x^2)$ series term by term
4 ptsPart (b): add enough terms; cite ASE Theorem
4 ptsPart (c): substitute $x=1$ and use $\arctan(1)=\pi/4$

Part (a)

$\dfrac{1}{1+x^2}=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty(-1)^n x^{2n}$ for $|x|\le1$ (geometric series with $r=-x^2$).

Integrate: $\arctan x=\int_0^x\dfrac{dt}{1+t^2}=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty(-1)^n\dfrac{x^{2n+1}}{2n+1}=x-\dfrac{x^3}{3}+\dfrac{x^5}{5}-\cdots$

Constant of integration is 0 since $\arctan(0)=0$.

Part (b)

At $x=0.5$ (alternating series): error $\le|a_{N+1}|=\dfrac{(0.5)^{2N+3}}{2N+3}$. Need this $<0.001$.

$N=1$: $\dfrac{(0.5)^5}{5}=\dfrac{0.03125}{5}=0.00625>0.001$. Try $N=2$: $\dfrac{(0.5)^7}{7}\approx0.000112<0.001$ ✓

$\arctan(0.5)\approx0.5-\dfrac{0.125}{3}+\dfrac{0.03125}{5}=0.5-0.04167+0.00625\approx\boxed{0.4646}$

(Actual: $\arctan(0.5)\approx0.4636$ rad. Our estimate has error $\approx0.0010<0.001$.)

Part (c)

At $x=1$ (the series converges by AST at $x=1$):

$\arctan(1)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty\dfrac{(-1)^n}{2n+1}=1-\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{1}{5}-\cdots$

Since $\arctan(1)=\dfrac{\pi}{4}$:

$$\dfrac{\pi}{4}=\sum_{n=0}^\infty\dfrac{(-1)^n}{2n+1}\implies\boxed{\pi=4\sum_{n=0}^\infty\dfrac{(-1)^n}{2n+1}}$$

This is the famous Leibniz formula for $\pi$.
