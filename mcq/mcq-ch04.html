A particle moves along a line with position $s(t) = t^3 - 6t^2$. At $t=2$, the particle is
A moving right and speeding up
B moving right and slowing down
C moving left and slowing down
D moving left and speeding up
$v(t)=3t^2-12t$; $v(2)=12-24=-12<0$ (moving left). $a(t)=6t-12$; $a(2)=0$. At $t=2$ acceleration is 0, but just after $t=2$: $a>0$ and $v<0$, meaning $|v|$ is decreasing — slowing down.
2
$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\dfrac{\sin 4x}{x} =$
A 4
B 1
C 0
D $\infty$
L'Hôpital (0/0): $\dfrac{4\cos 4x}{1}\Big|_{x=0}=4$. Or multiply and divide by 4: $4\cdot\dfrac{\sin4x}{4x}\to4\cdot1=4$.

The linearization of $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$ at $x=9$ is $L(x) =$
A $3 + 3(x-9)$
B $3 + \dfrac{1}{6}(x-9)$
C $3 + \dfrac{1}{3}(x-9)$
D $9 + \dfrac{1}{6}(x-9)$
$f(9)=3$; $f'(x)=\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$; $f'(9)=\dfrac{1}{6}$. $L(x)=3+\dfrac{1}{6}(x-9)$.

A ladder 10 ft long leans against a wall. When the base is 6 ft from the wall and sliding away at 2 ft/s, the top of the ladder slides down at
A $3/2$ ft/s
B $4$ ft/s
C $1$ ft/s
D $3/2$ ft/s
$x^2+y^2=100$. When $x=6$, $y=8$. Differentiate: $2x\dfrac{dx}{dt}+2y\dfrac{dy}{dt}=0$. $6(2)+8\dfrac{dy}{dt}=0\Rightarrow\dfrac{dy}{dt}=-\dfrac{3}{2}$ ft/s (downward).
5
$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{3x^2-5}{2x^2+1} =$
A 0
B $\infty$
C $3/2$
D $5$
Divide by $x^2$: $\dfrac{3-5/x^2}{2+1/x^2}\to\dfrac{3}{2}$ as $x\to\infty$.
6
$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\dfrac{x - \sin x}{x^3} =$
A $1/6$
B $1/3$
C $0$
D $1$
L'Hôpital 3×: $\dfrac{1-\cos x}{3x^2}\to\dfrac{\sin x}{6x}\to\dfrac{\cos x}{6}\Big|_{x=0}=\dfrac{1}{6}$.
7
A spherical balloon is inflated at $4\pi$ cm³/s. When the radius is 3 cm, the radius is growing at
A $4/9$ cm/s
B $1/9$ cm/s
C $4\pi/3$ cm/s
D $1/3$ cm/s
$V=\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3$; $\dfrac{dV}{dt}=4\pi r^2\dfrac{dr}{dt}$. $4\pi=4\pi(9)\dfrac{dr}{dt}\Rightarrow\dfrac{dr}{dt}=\dfrac{1}{9}$ cm/s.
8
The acceleration of a particle with position $s(t) = e^{2t}$ at $t=0$ is
A 1
B 2
C $e^2$
D 4
$v(t)=2e^{2t}$; $a(t)=4e^{2t}$; $a(0)=4e^0=4$.
9
$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0^+}x\ln x =$
A $1$
B $-1$
C $0$
D $\infty$
Form $0\cdot(-\infty)$. Rewrite: $\dfrac{\ln x}{1/x}$. L'Hôpital: $\dfrac{1/x}{-1/x^2}=-x\to0$.
10
Using linearization, approximate $\sqrt{4.08}$.
A 2.04
B 2.02
C 2.10
D 2.08
$L(x)=2+\dfrac{1}{4}(x-4)$ (since $f'(4)=\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{4}}=\dfrac{1}{4}$). $L(4.08)=2+\dfrac{0.08}{4}=2+0.02=2.02$.
11
A particle is at rest when $v(t)=0$. If $v(t) = t^2 - 4t + 3$, the particle is at rest at $t =$
A $1$ and $3$
B $2$ only
C $3$ only
D $0$ and $4$
$t^2-4t+3=(t-1)(t-3)=0\Rightarrow t=1$ or $t=3$.
12
$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\ln x}{x} =$
A $1$
B $e$
C $\infty$
D $0$
L'Hôpital ($\infty/\infty$): $\dfrac{1/x}{1}=\dfrac{1}{x}\to 0$.
13
Two cars start from the same point. Car A goes north at 60 mph; car B goes east at 80 mph. After 1 hour, the rate at which the distance between them increases is
A 70 mph
B 140 mph
C 100 mph
D 7200 mph
After 1 hr: $a=60$, $b=80$, $d=100$. $d^2=a^2+b^2$; $2d\dfrac{dd}{dt}=2a\dfrac{da}{dt}+2b\dfrac{db}{dt}$. $100\dfrac{dd}{dt}=60(60)+80(80)=3600+6400=10000\Rightarrow\dfrac{dd}{dt}=100$ mph.
14
$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\dfrac{e^x - 1 - x}{x^2} =$
A 0
B $1/2$
C 1
D $e$
L'Hôpital twice: $\dfrac{e^x-1}{2x}\to\dfrac{e^x}{2}\Big|_{x=0}=\dfrac{1}{2}$.
15
$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(1+\dfrac{1}{x}\right)^x =$
A $e$
B 1
C $\infty$
D 0
This is the definition of $e$: $\lim_{x\to\infty}\!\left(1+\dfrac{1}{x}\right)^x=e$.
16
The displacement of a particle from $t=0$ to $t=3$ with velocity $v(t) = t^2$ is
A $6$
B $6t$
C $27$
D $9$
Displacement $=\displaystyle\int_0^3 t^2\,dt = \left[\dfrac{t^3}{3}\right]_0^3 = 9$.
17
Water flows into a conical tank (vertex down, half-angle 30°) at $2$ m³/min. When depth is 3 m, the depth is rising at
A $\dfrac{2}{3\pi}$ m/min
B $\dfrac{2}{9\pi}$ m/min
C $\dfrac{2}{3\pi}$ m/min
D $\dfrac{1}{3\pi}$ m/min
$r=h\tan30°=h/\sqrt{3}$. $V=\dfrac{\pi}{3}r^2h=\dfrac{\pi h^3}{9}$. $\dfrac{dV}{dt}=\dfrac{\pi h^2}{3}\dfrac{dh}{dt}$. $2=\dfrac{9\pi}{3}\dfrac{dh}{dt}\Rightarrow\dfrac{dh}{dt}=\dfrac{2}{3\pi}$ m/min.
18
Which form is NOT a valid indeterminate form for L'Hôpital's Rule?
A $0/0$
B $0/\infty$
C $\infty/\infty$
D $0\cdot\infty$
$0/\infty$ is NOT indeterminate — it evaluates to 0 directly. L'Hôpital applies to $0/0$ and $\infty/\infty$. $0\cdot\infty$ can be converted to one of those forms.
19
A particle moves so that $v(t)>0$ and $a(t)<0$ for $t>0$. The particle is
A moving right and slowing down
B moving right and speeding up
C moving left and speeding up
D moving left and slowing down
$v>0$ means moving right. $v$ and $a$ have opposite signs, so speed $|v|$ is decreasing — slowing down.

$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0^+} x^x =$
A $0$
B $e$
C $\infty$
D $1$
$\ln(x^x)=x\ln x\to 0$ (as shown earlier). So $x^x=e^{x\ln x}\to e^0=1$.
