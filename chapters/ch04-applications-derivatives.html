4.1 Rates of Change in Context

If $f(x)$ models a quantity, then $f'(x)$ is the instantaneous rate of change of that quantity with respect to $x$.

📐 Velocity and Acceleration
For position function $s(t)$: $$v(t) = s'(t) \quad \text{(velocity)} \qquad a(t) = v'(t) = s''(t) \quad \text{(acceleration)}$$
  • Object moves right/up when $v(t) > 0$; moves left/down when $v(t) < 0$.
  • Object speeds up when $v$ and $a$ have the same sign; slows down when they have opposite signs.
  • Speed $= |v(t)|$ (always non-negative).
📝 Example
A particle has position $s(t) = t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t$ (meters, $t$ in seconds).
  • $v(t) = 3t^2 - 12t + 9 = 3(t-1)(t-3)$
  • $a(t) = 6t - 12$
  • At $t=2$: $v = 3(1)(-1) = -3$ m/s (moving left), $a = 0$.

4.2 Linear Approximation & Linearization

Near $x = a$, any differentiable function looks like its tangent line. This is local linear approximation.

📐 Linearization
The linearization of $f$ at $a$: $$L(x) = f(a) + f'(a)(x - a)$$ For $x$ near $a$: $f(x) \approx L(x)$.
📝 Example — Approximating $\sqrt{4.1}$
Use $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$, $a = 4$.
$f(4) = 2$, $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$, $f'(4) = \dfrac{1}{4}$.
$L(x) = 2 + \dfrac{1}{4}(x-4)$, so $\sqrt{4.1} \approx 2 + \dfrac{1}{4}(0.1) = 2.025$. (Actual: $\approx 2.02485$.)

4.3 Related Rates

Related rates problems involve two or more quantities that both depend on time $t$. Differentiate the relating equation implicitly with respect to $t$.

📐 Strategy for Related Rates
  1. Draw a diagram and label variables.
  2. Write an equation relating the variables.
  3. Differentiate both sides with respect to $t$ (using chain rule).
  4. Substitute known values and solve for the unknown rate.
⚠️ Common Error
Do NOT substitute specific values for the variables before differentiating — the values are only valid at a single instant, but the differentiated equation must hold generally.

Common Geometric Formulas Used in Related Rates

ShapeFormula
Circle area$A = \pi r^2 \;\Rightarrow\; \dfrac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi r \dfrac{dr}{dt}$
Sphere volume$V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \;\Rightarrow\; \dfrac{dV}{dt} = 4\pi r^2 \dfrac{dr}{dt}$
Cone volume$V = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$
Pythagorean$z^2 = x^2 + y^2 \;\Rightarrow\; z\dfrac{dz}{dt} = x\dfrac{dx}{dt} + y\dfrac{dy}{dt}$

4.4 L'Hôpital's Rule

✅ L'Hôpital's Rule
If $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to a}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}$ gives the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$ or $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$, and if $f$ and $g$ are differentiable near $a$ with $g'(x) \ne 0$, then: $$\lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}$$ (provided the right-hand limit exists or is $\pm\infty$). May be applied repeatedly.
⚠️ Only for Indeterminate Forms
L'Hôpital's Rule applies ONLY to $\dfrac{0}{0}$ or $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. Do not apply it when the limit is already determinate.

Other Indeterminate Forms — Rewrite First

FormRewrite as
$0 \cdot \infty$$\dfrac{f}{1/g}$ or $\dfrac{g}{1/f}$ → $\dfrac{0}{0}$ or $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$
$\infty - \infty$Common denominator or factor
$1^\infty$, $0^0$, $\infty^0$Take $\ln$: $\lim \ln y = \lim [g\ln f]$, then $y = e^{\lim\ln y}$

Worked Examples

Example 1Velocity and Acceleration

A particle moves along a line with position $s(t) = 2t^3 - 9t^2 + 12t$ (ft, $t$ in sec). Find when the particle is at rest, the velocity at $t=1$, and when it's speeding up on $[0,4]$.

1
$v(t) = 6t^2 - 18t + 12 = 6(t^2 - 3t + 2) = 6(t-1)(t-2)$. At rest: $t = 1$ and $t = 2$.
2
$v(1) = 6(0)(-1) = 0$ ft/s.
3
$a(t) = 12t - 18$. Sign analysis: $v > 0$ on $(0,1)\cup(2,4)$; $v < 0$ on $(1,2)$. $a > 0$ when $t > 3/2$; $a < 0$ when $t < 3/2$. Speeding up when same sign: $(1, 3/2)$ both negative, and $(2, 4)$ both positive. Speeding up on $(1, \frac{3}{2})$ and $(2, 4)$.
Example 2Related Rates — Ladder Problem

A 10-ft ladder leans against a wall. The base slides away at 2 ft/sec. How fast is the top of the ladder sliding down when the base is 6 ft from the wall?

1
Let $x$ = distance of base from wall, $y$ = height of top. Pythagorean: $x^2 + y^2 = 100$.
2
Differentiate w.r.t. $t$: $2x\dfrac{dx}{dt} + 2y\dfrac{dy}{dt} = 0$, so $\dfrac{dy}{dt} = -\dfrac{x}{y}\dfrac{dx}{dt}$.
3
At $x=6$: $y = \sqrt{100-36} = 8$. Given $\dfrac{dx}{dt} = 2$: $$\frac{dy}{dt} = -\frac{6}{8} \cdot 2 = \boxed{-\tfrac{3}{2} \text{ ft/sec}}$$ The negative means the top is sliding down.
Example 3Related Rates — Expanding Circle

A stone dropped in a pond creates circular ripples. The radius grows at 4 cm/s. How fast is the area increasing when the radius is 10 cm?

1
$A = \pi r^2$, $\dfrac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi r \dfrac{dr}{dt}$.
2
At $r=10$, $\dfrac{dr}{dt}=4$: $\dfrac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi(10)(4) = \boxed{80\pi \approx 251.3 \text{ cm}^2/\text{s}}$
Example 4L'Hôpital's Rule — $0/0$

Evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{e^x - 1 - x}{x^2}$.

1
Direct sub gives $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Apply L'Hôpital: $\dfrac{e^x - 1}{2x}$. Still $\dfrac{0}{0}$ at $x=0$.
2
Apply again: $\dfrac{e^x}{2}$. At $x=0$: $\boxed{\dfrac{1}{2}}$.
Example 5L'Hôpital's Rule — $\infty/\infty$

Evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\ln x}{x}$.

1
Form: $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. Apply L'Hôpital: $\dfrac{1/x}{1} = \dfrac{1}{x} \to 0$ as $x\to\infty$. So $\boxed{0}$.
Example 6L'Hôpital — $1^\infty$ Form

Evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(1 + \frac{1}{x}\right)^x$.

1
Form $1^\infty$. Let $y = \left(1+\dfrac{1}{x}\right)^x$. Then $\ln y = x\ln\!\left(1+\dfrac{1}{x}\right)$, which is $\infty \cdot 0$.
2
Rewrite: $\ln y = \dfrac{\ln(1+1/x)}{1/x}$ — form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Apply L'Hôpital: $$= \frac{\tfrac{-1/x^2}{1+1/x}}{-1/x^2} = \frac{1}{1+1/x} \to 1 \text{ as } x\to\infty$$
3
So $\ln y \to 1$, meaning $y \to e^1 = \boxed{e}$. (This is the classic definition of $e$.)
Example 7Related Rates — Water Tank (Cone)

Water drains from a conical tank (vertex down) with height 12 m and radius 4 m. Water leaves at 2 m³/min. How fast is the water level dropping when the depth is 6 m?

1
Similar triangles: $\dfrac{r}{h} = \dfrac{4}{12} = \dfrac{1}{3}$, so $r = \dfrac{h}{3}$.
2
$V = \dfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h = \dfrac{1}{3}\pi\left(\dfrac{h}{3}\right)^2 h = \dfrac{\pi h^3}{27}$
3
Differentiate: $\dfrac{dV}{dt} = \dfrac{\pi h^2}{9}\dfrac{dh}{dt}$.
4
At $h=6$, $\dfrac{dV}{dt} = -2$: $$-2 = \frac{\pi(36)}{9}\frac{dh}{dt} \implies \frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{-2}{4\pi} = \boxed{-\frac{1}{2\pi} \approx -0.159 \text{ m/min}}$$
