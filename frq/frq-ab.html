FRQ 1 Limits — Algebraic Evaluation AB

Evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 3}\dfrac{x^2-9}{x-3}$ and justify your answer. Then determine whether $f(x)=\dfrac{x^2-9}{x-3}$ is continuous at $x=3$. If not, classify the discontinuity.

Scoring Rubric (9 points total)
2 ptsFactor numerator: $x^2-9=(x-3)(x+3)$
2 ptsCancel $(x-3)$ and evaluate limit: $\lim_{x\to3}(x+3)=6$
2 ptsNote $f(3)$ is undefined (0/0 form)
3 ptsCorrectly classify as removable discontinuity at $x=3$, because the limit exists but $f(3)$ is undefined

Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1 — Factor:

$$\frac{x^2-9}{x-3} = \frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x-3}$$

Step 2 — Cancel $(x-3)$ (valid since we are taking the limit as $x\to3$, not setting $x=3$):

$$\lim_{x\to 3}\frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x-3} = \lim_{x\to 3}(x+3) = 3+3 = \boxed{6}$$

Step 3 — Check continuity: For continuity at $x=3$ we need (1) $f(3)$ defined, (2) limit exists, (3) both equal. Here $f(3)=9/0$ is undefined, so $f$ is NOT continuous at $x=3$.

Step 4 — Classify: Since $\lim_{x\to3}f(x)=6$ exists but $f(3)$ is undefined, this is a removable discontinuity (hole at $(3,6)$).

Graph of $f(x)=\frac{x^2-9}{x-3}$ showing the hole at $(3, 6)$.

FRQ 2 Limits at Infinity / Horizontal Asymptotes AB

Let $f(x)=\dfrac{3x^3-2x+1}{5x^3+4x^2}$. Find $\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)$ and $\lim_{x\to-\infty}f(x)$. Identify all horizontal asymptotes.

Scoring Rubric (6 points)
2 ptsDivide numerator and denominator by $x^3$
2 ptsEvaluate both limits correctly as $3/5$
2 ptsState: one horizontal asymptote $y=3/5$

Solution

Divide every term by $x^3$ (highest power):

$$\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}\frac{3-2/x^2+1/x^3}{5+4/x} = \frac{3-0+0}{5+0} = \frac{3}{5}$$

Both limits equal $3/5$, so there is exactly one horizontal asymptote: $\boxed{y=\dfrac{3}{5}}$.

FRQ 3 Derivative from the Limit Definition AB

Using the limit definition of the derivative, find $f'(x)$ for $f(x) = 2x^2 - 3x$. Verify your result using the power rule.

Scoring Rubric (8 points)
2 ptsCorrectly write $\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}$
3 ptsExpand $f(x+h)$, simplify numerator, and factor out $h$
2 ptsEvaluate limit correctly: $f'(x)=4x-3$
1 ptPower rule verification

Solution

$$f'(x)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}$$

Compute $f(x+h)=2(x+h)^2-3(x+h)=2x^2+4xh+2h^2-3x-3h$.

$$f(x+h)-f(x)=4xh+2h^2-3h$$

$$\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}=\frac{h(4x+2h-3)}{h}=4x+2h-3$$

$$f'(x)=\lim_{h\to0}(4x+2h-3)=\boxed{4x-3}$$

Verification: Power rule: $f'(x)=2\cdot2x^{2-1}-3\cdot1=4x-3$. ✓

FRQ 4 Chain Rule & Product Rule AB

Find the derivative of $g(x) = (3x^2+1)^4 \cdot e^{2x}$. Simplify your answer by factoring out common terms.

Scoring Rubric (7 points)
2 ptsCorrectly identify product rule structure
2 ptsDifferentiate $(3x^2+1)^4$ using chain rule: $4(3x^2+1)^3\cdot6x$
1 ptDifferentiate $e^{2x}$ using chain rule: $2e^{2x}$
2 ptsCorrectly combine and factor

Solution

Product rule: $g'=u'v+uv'$ with $u=(3x^2+1)^4$, $v=e^{2x}$.

$u'=4(3x^2+1)^3\cdot 6x = 24x(3x^2+1)^3$

$v'=2e^{2x}$

$$g'=24x(3x^2+1)^3e^{2x} + (3x^2+1)^4\cdot2e^{2x}$$

Factor out $2e^{2x}(3x^2+1)^3$:

$$g'=2e^{2x}(3x^2+1)^3\left[12x+(3x^2+1)\right]=\boxed{2e^{2x}(3x^2+1)^3(3x^2+12x+1)}$$

FRQ 5 Implicit Differentiation — Tangent Line AB

The curve $x^3 + y^3 = 9$ passes through $(1, 2)$. Use implicit differentiation to find $dy/dx$, then write the equation of the tangent line at $(1, 2)$.

Scoring Rubric (8 points)
2 ptsDifferentiate both sides with respect to $x$
2 ptsSolve for $dy/dx = -x^2/y^2$
2 ptsEvaluate slope at $(1,2)$: $m=-1/4$
2 ptsWrite tangent line equation

Solution

Differentiate $x^3+y^3=9$ implicitly:

$$3x^2+3y^2\frac{dy}{dx}=0 \implies \frac{dy}{dx}=-\frac{x^2}{y^2}$$

At $(1,2)$: $m=-\dfrac{1^2}{2^2}=-\dfrac{1}{4}$

Tangent line: $y-2=-\dfrac{1}{4}(x-1)$

$$\boxed{y=-\frac{1}{4}x+\frac{9}{4}}$$

FRQ 6 Related Rates — Cone AB

Water drains from a conical tank (vertex down) whose height is 10 m and base radius is 5 m at a rate of $3$ m³/min. How fast is the water level falling when the water is 6 m deep?

Scoring Rubric (9 points)
2 ptsEstablish similar triangles: $r/h=1/2$, so $r=h/2$
2 ptsWrite $V=\frac{\pi}{12}h^3$ and differentiate
3 ptsSubstitute $dV/dt=-3$, $h=6$ and solve for $dh/dt$
2 ptsCorrect answer with units

Solution

Conical tank: similar triangles give $r = h/2$.

Similar triangles: $\dfrac{r}{h}=\dfrac{5}{10}=\dfrac{1}{2}$, so $r=\dfrac{h}{2}$.

Volume: $V=\dfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h=\dfrac{1}{3}\pi\dfrac{h^2}{4}h=\dfrac{\pi h^3}{12}$

Differentiate: $\dfrac{dV}{dt}=\dfrac{\pi h^2}{4}\dfrac{dh}{dt}$

Water drains, so $\dfrac{dV}{dt}=-3$. At $h=6$:

$$-3=\dfrac{\pi(36)}{4}\dfrac{dh}{dt}=9\pi\dfrac{dh}{dt}$$

$$\dfrac{dh}{dt}=\dfrac{-3}{9\pi}=\boxed{-\dfrac{1}{3\pi}\approx-0.106 \text{ m/min}}$$

The water level is falling at $\dfrac{1}{3\pi}$ m/min.

FRQ 8 Mean Value Theorem AB

Let $f(x)=x^3-x$ on $[-1,2]$. Verify the hypotheses of the MVT are satisfied, then find all values of $c$ guaranteed by the theorem.

Scoring Rubric (7 points)
2 ptsVerify continuity on $[-1,2]$ and differentiability on $(-1,2)$ (polynomial)
2 ptsCompute average rate of change: $[f(2)-f(-1)]/(2-(-1))$
3 ptsSolve $f'(c)=$ average rate and find valid $c$ values

Solution

$f(x)=x^3-x$ is a polynomial → continuous on $[-1,2]$ and differentiable on $(-1,2)$. MVT applies.

$f(2)=8-2=6$; $f(-1)=-1+1=0$.

Average rate $= \dfrac{6-0}{2-(-1)}=\dfrac{6}{3}=2$

$3c^2-1=2\Rightarrow3c^2=3\Rightarrow c^2=1\Rightarrow c=\pm1$

(a) Estimate $\int_0^8 f(x)\,dx$ using a right Riemann sum with 4 equal subintervals. (b) Is this an over- or underestimate if $f$ is increasing on $[0,8]$?

Scoring Rubric (7 points)
3 ptsPart (a): $\Delta x=2$; use $f(2)+f(4)+f(6)+f(8)$
2 ptsPart (a): correct sum $=34$
2 ptsPart (b): overestimate if $f$ increasing (right sum ≥ true value)

Solution

(a) $\Delta x=2$, subintervals $[0,2],[2,4],[4,6],[6,8]$. Right endpoints: $x=2,4,6,8$.

$$\int_0^8 f\,dx\approx2[f(2)+f(4)+f(6)+f(8)]=2[5+3+7+2]=2(17)=\boxed{34}$$

(b) For an increasing function, $f$ takes its maximum on each subinterval at the right endpoint. So the right Riemann sum is an overestimate when $f$ is increasing.

FRQ 25 Mixed — Comprehensive AB Problem AB

Let $f(x)=\ln(x^2+1)$.

(a) Find $f'(x)$ and $f''(x)$.

(b) Find all intervals where $f$ is increasing and all inflection points.

(c) Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^1\dfrac{x}{x^2+1}dx$.

Scoring Rubric (10 points)
3 ptsPart (a): $f'=2x/(x^2+1)$; $f''=(2-2x^2)/(x^2+1)^2$
3 ptsPart (b): increasing on $(0,\infty)$; inflection at $x=\pm1$
4 ptsPart (c): $u$-sub gives $\frac{1}{2}\ln2$

Part (a)

$f'(x)=\dfrac{2x}{x^2+1}$

$f''(x)=\dfrac{2(x^2+1)-2x\cdot2x}{(x^2+1)^2}=\dfrac{2-2x^2}{(x^2+1)^2}$

Part (b)

$f'(x)>0\Leftrightarrow x>0$: increasing on $(0,\infty)$; decreasing on $(-\infty,0)$. Local minimum at $x=0$: $f(0)=0$.

$f''(x)=0\Rightarrow 2-2x^2=0\Rightarrow x=\pm1$. $f''$ changes sign at both $x=\pm1$ (inflection points).

$f(\pm1)=\ln2$. Inflection points: $(-1,\ln2)$ and $(1,\ln2)$.

Part (c)

$u=x^2+1$, $du=2x\,dx$, limits: $u(0)=1$, $u(1)=2$.

$$\int_0^1\frac{x}{x^2+1}dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_1^2\frac{du}{u}=\frac{1}{2}[\ln u]_1^2=\frac{1}{2}(\ln2-0)=\boxed{\frac{\ln2}{2}}$$
