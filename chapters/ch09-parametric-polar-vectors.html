9.1 Parametric Equations

A parametric curve is defined by expressing $x$ and $y$ as functions of a parameter $t$:

Figure 9.1 Parametric curves. The parameter $t$ is a clock: each value gives a point, and increasing $t$ gives the direction of travel.
$$x = f(t), \qquad y = g(t), \qquad t \in [a, b]$$
📌 Key Difference from Cartesian
Parametric equations encode not just the shape of a curve, but also the direction of motion and speed as $t$ increases. The same curve can be traversed in different directions depending on how $x$ and $y$ are parameterized.

Eliminating the Parameter

Solve one equation for $t$, substitute into the other to get a Cartesian equation.

📝 Example
$x = 2t$, $y = t^2 - 1$. From first: $t = x/2$. Sub: $y = (x/2)^2 - 1 = \dfrac{x^2}{4} - 1$ (parabola).

9.2 Calculus with Parametric Equations

✅ First Derivative (Slope)
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt} \qquad \text{(provided } dx/dt \ne 0\text{)}$$
✅ Second Derivative
$$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \frac{d}{dx}\!\left[\frac{dy}{dx}\right] = \frac{\dfrac{d}{dt}\!\left[\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right]}{dx/dt}$$
✅ Arc Length (Parametric)
$$L = \int_a^b \sqrt{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2}\,dt$$
✅ Area under Parametric Curve
$$A = \int_a^b y\,\frac{dx}{dt}\,dt \qquad \text{(when curve is traced left-to-right)}$$

Horizontal and Vertical Tangents

  • Horizontal tangent: $\dfrac{dy}{dt} = 0$ and $\dfrac{dx}{dt} \ne 0$
  • Vertical tangent: $\dfrac{dx}{dt} = 0$ and $\dfrac{dy}{dt} \ne 0$

9.3 Polar Coordinates

In polar coordinates, a point is described by $(r, \theta)$ where $r$ is the distance from the origin and $\theta$ is the angle from the positive $x$-axis.

Figure 9.2 Polar curves. Plot distance $r$ from the origin at each angle $\theta$. Area uses $A=\tfrac12\int_\alpha^\beta r^2\,d\theta$.
📐 Conversion Formulas
Polar → Cartesian:

$x = r\cos\theta$, $y = r\sin\theta$

Cartesian → Polar:

$r^2 = x^2 + y^2$, $\tan\theta = y/x$

Common Polar Curves

CurveEquationShape
Circle$r = a$Circle of radius $a$
Cardioid$r = a(1 + \cos\theta)$Heart-shaped curve
Rose (3 petals)$r = a\cos(3\theta)$Three-petaled rose
Rose (4 petals)$r = a\cos(2\theta)$Four-petaled rose
Lemniscate$r^2 = a^2\cos(2\theta)$Figure-eight
Limaçon$r = a + b\cos\theta$Varies by $a/b$ ratio

9.4 Calculus with Polar Equations

✅ Slope of Polar Curve
Using $x = r\cos\theta$, $y = r\sin\theta$: $$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/d\theta}{dx/d\theta} = \frac{r'(\theta)\sin\theta + r(\theta)\cos\theta}{r'(\theta)\cos\theta - r(\theta)\sin\theta}$$
✅ Polar Area
Area of the region swept from $\alpha$ to $\beta$ by $r = f(\theta)$: $$A = \frac{1}{2}\int_\alpha^\beta [r(\theta)]^2\,d\theta$$ Area between two curves ($r_{\text{outer}} \ge r_{\text{inner}}$): $$A = \frac{1}{2}\int_\alpha^\beta\left([r_{\text{outer}}]^2 - [r_{\text{inner}}]^2\right)d\theta$$
Figure 9.3 Polar area. Sweep a thin sector of angle $d\theta$ and radius $r$: its area is $\tfrac12r^2\,d\theta$. Integrate over the angle range for the full region.

9.5 Vector-Valued Functions

📐 Vector-Valued Function
$$\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle x(t),\, y(t) \rangle = x(t)\,\mathbf{i} + y(t)\,\mathbf{j}$$ This is essentially a parametric curve expressed as a vector.
✅ Calculus of Vector Functions
QuantityFormula
Velocity vector$\mathbf{v}(t) = \mathbf{r}'(t) = \langle x'(t), y'(t) \rangle$
Speed$|\mathbf{v}(t)| = \sqrt{[x'(t)]^2 + [y'(t)]^2}$
Acceleration vector$\mathbf{a}(t) = \mathbf{v}'(t) = \langle x''(t), y''(t) \rangle$
Position from velocity$\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{r}(t_0) + \displaystyle\int_{t_0}^t \mathbf{v}(\tau)\,d\tau$
Total distance$\displaystyle\int_a^b |\mathbf{v}(t)|\,dt = \int_a^b\sqrt{[x']^2+[y']^2}\,dt$
📌 Displacement vs. Distance
Displacement $= |\mathbf{r}(b) - \mathbf{r}(a)|$ — straight-line distance from start to end.
Total distance traveled $= \displaystyle\int_a^b|\mathbf{v}(t)|\,dt$ — actual path length.

Worked Examples

Example 1Parametric Slope and Tangent

Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ and the equation of the tangent at $t=1$ for $x = t^2 + 1$, $y = t^3 - t$.

1
$\dfrac{dx}{dt} = 2t$, $\dfrac{dy}{dt} = 3t^2 - 1$. So $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{3t^2-1}{2t}$.
2
At $t=1$: $x=2$, $y=0$. Slope $= \dfrac{3-1}{2} = 1$.
3
Tangent: $y - 0 = 1(x-2)$, so $\boxed{y = x - 2}$.
Example 2Parametric Arc Length

Find the length of the curve $x = 3t^2$, $y = 2t^3$ from $t=0$ to $t=1$.

1
$\dfrac{dx}{dt}=6t$, $\dfrac{dy}{dt}=6t^2$. $(dx/dt)^2+(dy/dt)^2 = 36t^2+36t^4=36t^2(1+t^2)$.
2
$L = \displaystyle\int_0^1 6t\sqrt{1+t^2}\,dt$. Let $u=1+t^2$, $du=2t\,dt$: $= 3\displaystyle\int_1^2 \sqrt{u}\,du = 3\cdot\dfrac{2}{3}u^{3/2}\Big|_1^2 = 2(2^{3/2}-1) = \boxed{4\sqrt{2}-2}$
Example 3Polar to Cartesian

Convert $r = 4\cos\theta$ to Cartesian form and identify the curve.

1
Multiply both sides by $r$: $r^2 = 4r\cos\theta \Rightarrow x^2 + y^2 = 4x$.
2
Complete the square: $(x-2)^2 + y^2 = 4$. This is a circle centered at $(2,0)$ with radius 2.
Example 4Polar Area — Cardioid

Find the area enclosed by the cardioid $r = 2(1+\cos\theta)$.

1
$A = \dfrac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}[2(1+\cos\theta)]^2\,d\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}\int_0^{2\pi}4(1+\cos\theta)^2\,d\theta = 2\int_0^{2\pi}(1+2\cos\theta+\cos^2\theta)\,d\theta$
2
Use $\cos^2\theta = \dfrac{1+\cos 2\theta}{2}$: $= 2\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\!\left(\dfrac{3}{2}+2\cos\theta+\dfrac{\cos 2\theta}{2}\right)d\theta$
3
$= 2\left[\dfrac{3\theta}{2}+2\sin\theta+\dfrac{\sin 2\theta}{4}\right]_0^{2\pi} = 2\cdot 3\pi = \boxed{6\pi}$
Example 5Polar Area — Between Curves

Find the area inside $r = 3\cos\theta$ and outside $r = 1 + \cos\theta$.

1
Intersections: $3\cos\theta = 1+\cos\theta \Rightarrow 2\cos\theta = 1 \Rightarrow \theta = \pm\pi/3$.
2
On $[-\pi/3, \pi/3]$: $3\cos\theta \ge 1+\cos\theta$ (check $\theta=0$: $3\ge 2$ ✓).
3
$A = \dfrac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{-\pi/3}^{\pi/3}[(3\cos\theta)^2-(1+\cos\theta)^2]\,d\theta$ $= \dfrac{1}{2}\int_{-\pi/3}^{\pi/3}[9\cos^2\theta - 1 - 2\cos\theta - \cos^2\theta]\,d\theta$ $= \dfrac{1}{2}\int_{-\pi/3}^{\pi/3}[8\cos^2\theta - 2\cos\theta - 1]\,d\theta$
4
Use $\cos^2\theta = (1+\cos 2\theta)/2$ and evaluate: $= \boxed{\pi}$
Example 6Vector-Valued Function

A particle has velocity $\mathbf{v}(t) = \langle 2t, 3t^2 \rangle$ and position $\mathbf{r}(0) = \langle 1, -1 \rangle$. Find $\mathbf{r}(2)$ and the total distance from $t=0$ to $t=2$.

1
Integrate: $\mathbf{r}(t) = \left\langle t^2+C_1,\, t^3+C_2 \right\rangle$. Using $\mathbf{r}(0) = \langle 1,-1\rangle$: $C_1=1, C_2=-1$. So $\mathbf{r}(t)=\langle t^2+1, t^3-1\rangle$.
2
$\mathbf{r}(2) = \langle 5, 7\rangle$.
3
Speed $= |\mathbf{v}(t)| = \sqrt{4t^2+9t^4} = t\sqrt{4+9t^2}$. Distance $= \displaystyle\int_0^2 t\sqrt{4+9t^2}\,dt$. Let $u=4+9t^2$, $du=18t\,dt$: $= \dfrac{1}{18}\displaystyle\int_4^{40}\sqrt{u}\,du = \dfrac{1}{18}\cdot\dfrac{2}{3}u^{3/2}\Big|_4^{40} = \dfrac{1}{27}(40^{3/2}-8) = \dfrac{1}{27}(80\sqrt{10}-8) = \boxed{\dfrac{8(10\sqrt{10}-1)}{27}}$
