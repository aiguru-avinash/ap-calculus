7.1 Introduction to Differential Equations

A differential equation (DE) is an equation involving an unknown function and one or more of its derivatives.

📐 Terminology
  • Order: the order of the highest derivative in the equation.
  • General solution: a family of solutions containing arbitrary constants.
  • Particular solution: a specific solution satisfying an initial condition.
  • Initial condition: a specified value $y(x_0) = y_0$ used to determine the constant.
📝 Example
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2x$ is a first-order DE. General solution: $y = x^2 + C$. With $y(0)=3$: particular solution $y = x^2 + 3$.

7.2 Slope Fields

A slope field (direction field) is a visual representation of a DE $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = f(x,y)$. At each point $(x,y)$, draw a short line segment with slope $f(x,y)$.

Figure 7.1 — Slope field for $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = y$. Slope at each point equals its $y$-value. Solution curves are exponentials $y = Ce^x$.

7.3 Euler's Method BC

Euler's method numerically approximates a solution to $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = f(x,y)$ with initial condition $y(x_0) = y_0$ using step size $h$:

📐 Euler's Method
$$x_{n+1} = x_n + h \qquad y_{n+1} = y_n + h \cdot f(x_n, y_n)$$ Each step follows the tangent line at the current point.
⚠️ Accuracy
Smaller step size $h$ gives more accuracy. Euler's method overestimates on concave-down portions and underestimates on concave-up portions.

7.4 Separation of Variables

📐 Separable Differential Equation
A DE of the form $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = g(x)h(y)$ can be solved by:
  1. Separate: $\dfrac{dy}{h(y)} = g(x)\,dx$
  2. Integrate both sides: $\displaystyle\int \frac{dy}{h(y)} = \int g(x)\,dx$
  3. Solve for $y$ (if possible); apply initial condition to find $C$.

7.5 Exponential Growth and Decay

✅ Exponential Growth/Decay Model
The DE $\dfrac{dy}{dt} = ky$ (where $k$ is a constant) has general solution: $$y(t) = y_0 e^{kt}$$ where $y_0 = y(0)$ is the initial value.
  • $k > 0$: exponential growth
  • $k < 0$: exponential decay

Half-Life and Doubling Time

📌 Formulas
Half-life $T_{1/2}$: time for quantity to halve. $e^{kT_{1/2}} = \dfrac{1}{2} \Rightarrow T_{1/2} = \dfrac{\ln 2}{|k|}$

Doubling time $T_2$: $e^{kT_2} = 2 \Rightarrow T_2 = \dfrac{\ln 2}{k}$ ($k > 0$).

7.6 Logistic Growth BC

📐 Logistic Differential Equation
$$\frac{dP}{dt} = kP\!\left(1 - \frac{P}{M}\right)$$ where $M$ is the carrying capacity (maximum sustainable population). Solution: $P(t) = \dfrac{M}{1 + Ae^{-kt}}$ where $A = \dfrac{M - P_0}{P_0}$.
📌 Key Features of Logistic Growth
  • Growth is fastest when $P = M/2$ (inflection point of the solution curve).
  • $P \to M$ as $t \to \infty$ (approaches carrying capacity asymptotically).
  • $\dfrac{dP}{dt}$ is maximized when $P = M/2$, giving max rate $= kM/4$.

Worked Examples

Example 1Separable DE with Initial Condition

Solve $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = xy^2$ with $y(0) = 2$.

1
Separate: $\dfrac{dy}{y^2} = x\,dx$, i.e., $y^{-2}\,dy = x\,dx$.
2
Integrate: $-\dfrac{1}{y} = \dfrac{x^2}{2} + C$.
3
Apply $y(0)=2$: $-\dfrac{1}{2} = 0 + C$, so $C = -\dfrac{1}{2}$.
4
Solve for $y$: $-\dfrac{1}{y} = \dfrac{x^2}{2} - \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{x^2-1}{2}$, so $\boxed{y = \dfrac{-2}{x^2-1} = \dfrac{2}{1-x^2}}$ (for $|x|<1$).
Example 2Exponential Decay — Radioactive

A radioactive substance has a half-life of 10 years. Starting with 200 grams, how much remains after 25 years?

1
$T_{1/2} = 10 \Rightarrow k = -\dfrac{\ln 2}{10}$.
2
$y(t) = 200\,e^{-(\ln 2/10)t}$. At $t=25$: $$y(25) = 200\,e^{-2.5\ln 2} = 200 \cdot 2^{-2.5} = \frac{200}{4\sqrt{2}} = \frac{50}{\sqrt{2}} \approx \boxed{35.4 \text{ grams}}$$
Example 3Euler's Method (BC)

Use Euler's method with $h=0.1$ and two steps to approximate $y(0.2)$ for $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = x + y$, $y(0) = 1$.

1
Step 1: $(x_0, y_0) = (0, 1)$. $f(0,1) = 0+1=1$. $x_1 = 0.1$, $y_1 = 1 + 0.1(1) = 1.1$.
2
Step 2: $(x_1, y_1) = (0.1, 1.1)$. $f(0.1, 1.1) = 0.1+1.1=1.2$. $x_2 = 0.2$, $y_2 = 1.1 + 0.1(1.2) = 1.1 + 0.12 = \boxed{1.22}$.
Example 4Slope Field Matching

Explain how to identify the slope field for $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = x - y$ and sketch a solution through $(0, 0)$.

1
At $(0,0)$: slope $= 0-0 = 0$ (horizontal). At $(1,0)$: slope $= 1-0 = 1$. At $(0,1)$: slope $= 0-1 = -1$. Slopes are zero along the line $y=x$ (the isocline for slope 0).
2
The solution through $(0,0)$: The DE $\dfrac{dy}{dx} + y = x$ is linear. Solution is $y = x - 1 + e^{-x}$. At $x=0$: $y = 0-1+1=0$ ✓. Near origin, the curve rises at slope 0, then follows $y \approx x-1$ for large $x$.
Example 5Logistic Growth (BC)

A population satisfies $\dfrac{dP}{dt} = 0.04P\!\left(1 - \dfrac{P}{1000}\right)$ with $P(0) = 100$. When is growth fastest? What is the carrying capacity?

1
Carrying capacity $M = \boxed{1000}$.
2
Growth is fastest when $P = M/2 = \boxed{500}$. Maximum rate $= 0.04 \cdot 500\!\left(1 - \dfrac{500}{1000}\right) = 0.04 \cdot 500 \cdot 0.5 = 10$ per unit time.
Example 6Newton's Law of Cooling

A cup of coffee at 90°C cools in a 20°C room. After 5 minutes it's 70°C. When will it reach 40°C?

1
Newton's law: $\dfrac{dT}{dt} = k(T-20)$. Solution: $T - 20 = (T_0 - 20)e^{kt} = 70e^{kt}$.
2
At $t=5$, $T=70$: $50 = 70e^{5k} \Rightarrow e^{5k} = 5/7 \Rightarrow k = \dfrac{\ln(5/7)}{5} \approx -0.0673$.
3
For $T=40$: $20 = 70e^{kt} \Rightarrow e^{kt} = \dfrac{2}{7} \Rightarrow t = \dfrac{\ln(2/7)}{k} = \dfrac{\ln(2/7)}{\ln(5/7)/5} = \dfrac{5\ln(2/7)}{\ln(5/7)} \approx \boxed{19.4 \text{ min}}$
