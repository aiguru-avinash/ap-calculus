The geometric series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^n$ converges to
A $1$
B $2$
C $3/2$
D $3$

2
The Divergence Test says: if $\lim_{n\to\infty}a_n \ne 0$, then $\sum a_n$
A diverges
B converges
C may or may not converge
D converges absolutely
If $\lim a_n\ne0$, the series diverges. But $\lim a_n=0$ does NOT guarantee convergence (e.g., harmonic series).
3
The $p$-series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{n^p}$ converges if and only if
A $p < 1$
B $p \le 1$
C $p \ge 1$
D $p > 1$
$\sum 1/n^p$ converges $\Leftrightarrow p>1$. The harmonic series ($p=1$) diverges.
4
The Ratio Test for $\displaystyle\sum a_n$: if $\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|\dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right| = L < 1$, then
A the series diverges
B the series converges absolutely
C the test is inconclusive
D the series conditionally converges
Ratio Test: $L<1\Rightarrow$ absolute convergence; $L>1\Rightarrow$ divergence; $L=1\Rightarrow$ inconclusive.
5
The Maclaurin series for $e^x$ is
A $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(-1)^n x^n}{n!}$
B $1+x+\dfrac{x^2}{2}+\dfrac{x^3}{6}$
C $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{x^n}{n!}$
D $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{x^n}{n}$
$e^x=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty\dfrac{x^n}{n!}=1+x+\dfrac{x^2}{2!}+\cdots$ for all $x$. (Option B is just a partial sum, not the full series.)
6
The Alternating Series Test requires: terms decrease in absolute value AND
A $\lim_{n\to\infty}b_n = 0$
B $b_n$ is increasing
C $b_n > 1$ for all $n$
D the series is geometric
Alternating Series Test (Leibniz): $\sum(-1)^n b_n$ converges if (1) $b_{n+1}\le b_n$ (decreasing) and (2) $\lim b_n=0$.
7
The interval of convergence of $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty x^n$ is
A $(-\infty,\infty)$
B $[-1,1]$
C $(-1,1]$
D $(-1,1)$
$\sum x^n$ is geometric with $r=x$. Converges when $|x|<1$, i.e., $x\in(-1,1)$. Diverges at both endpoints.
8
The radius of convergence of $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty\dfrac{n!}{2^n}x^n$ is
A $\infty$
B $0$
C $2$
D $1/2$
Ratio Test: $\left|\dfrac{(n+1)!x^{n+1}/2^{n+1}}{n!x^n/2^n}\right|=\dfrac{(n+1)|x|}{2}\to\infty$ for any $x\ne0$. So $R=0$ (converges only at $x=0$).
9
The Maclaurin series for $\sin x$ is
A $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{x^{2n}}{(2n)!}$
B $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(-1)^n x^{2n}}{(2n)!}$
C $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(-1)^n x^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}$
D $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{x^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}$
$\sin x=x-\dfrac{x^3}{3!}+\dfrac{x^5}{5!}-\cdots=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty\dfrac{(-1)^n x^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}$. (Option B is $\cos x$.)

$e^{0.1}\approx 1 + 0.1 + 0.005 + 0.000167 = ?$ (using Maclaurin, 3 terms after 1)
A $1.10517$
B $1.1$
C $1.105$
D $1.115$

11
The harmonic series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{n}$
A converges to 1
B converges to $\ln 2$
C converges to $e$
D diverges
The harmonic series diverges (shown by integral test or grouping argument). It is the classic $p=1$ divergent $p$-series.
12
For the power series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty\dfrac{(x-2)^n}{3^n}$, the radius of convergence is
A 1
B 2
C 3
D $1/3$
Ratio Test: $\left|\dfrac{(x-2)^{n+1}/3^{n+1}}{(x-2)^n/3^n}\right|=\dfrac{|x-2|}{3}<1\Rightarrow|x-2|<3$. Radius $R=3$.
13
To find the Maclaurin series for $e^{-x^2}$, substitute into $e^x$'s series. The result is
A $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty\dfrac{x^{2n}}{n!}$
B $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty\dfrac{(-1)^n x^{2n}}{n!}$
C $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty\dfrac{(-x)^n}{n!}$
D $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty\dfrac{x^n}{n!}$
Replace $x$ with $-x^2$ in $\sum x^n/n!$: $\sum\dfrac{(-x^2)^n}{n!}=\sum\dfrac{(-1)^n x^{2n}}{n!}$.
14
$\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty(-1)^{n+1}\dfrac{1}{n} = 1 - \dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}-\cdots$ converges to
A $\ln 2$
B $1$
C $e-1$
D $\pi/4$
From $\ln(1+x)=x-x^2/2+x^3/3-\cdots$ at $x=1$: $\ln 2=1-1/2+1/3-\cdots$
15
The Lagrange error bound for $|f(x)-T_n(x)|$ uses the formula
A $\dfrac{M|x-a|^n}{n!}$
B $M|x-a|^{n+1}$
C $\dfrac{M}{(n+1)!}$
D $\dfrac{M|x-a|^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}$
Lagrange error: $|R_n(x)|\le\dfrac{M|x-a|^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}$, where $M$ bounds $|f^{(n+1)}|$ on the interval.
16
The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\dfrac{1}{n^2}$ converges by the
A Divergence Test
B Ratio Test
C $p$-series Test ($p=2>1$)
D Geometric Series Test
$\sum 1/n^2$ is a $p$-series with $p=2>1$, so it converges.
17
The Taylor series for $\cos x$ centered at $0$ is
A $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty\dfrac{(-1)^n x^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}$
B $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty\dfrac{(-1)^n x^{2n}}{(2n)!}$
C $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty\dfrac{x^{2n}}{(2n)!}$
D $1 - x^2 + x^4$
$\cos x=1-\dfrac{x^2}{2!}+\dfrac{x^4}{4!}-\cdots=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty\dfrac{(-1)^n x^{2n}}{(2n)!}$.
18
The series $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty\dfrac{(-1)^n}{2n+1} = 1-\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{1}{5}-\cdots$ converges to
A $\pi/4$
B $\pi/2$
C $\pi$
D $1$
From $\arctan x=x-x^3/3+x^5/5-\cdots$ at $x=1$: $\arctan(1)=\pi/4=1-1/3+1/5-\cdots$
19
If a series converges absolutely, it also
A converges conditionally only
B diverges
C may or may not converge
D converges
Absolute convergence implies convergence. The converse is false (conditionally convergent series converge but not absolutely).
