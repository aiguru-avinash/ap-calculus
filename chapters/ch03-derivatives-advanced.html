3.1 The Chain Rule

The chain rule handles derivatives of composite functions $f(g(x))$.

✅ Chain Rule
If $y = f(g(x))$, then: $$\frac{dy}{dx} = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)$$ In Leibniz notation: if $y = f(u)$ and $u = g(x)$, then $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{dy}{du} \cdot \dfrac{du}{dx}$
📌 Verbal Description
"Derivative of the outside (leaving inside unchanged) times derivative of the inside."

Examples at a glance:

FunctionOuter $f$Inner $g$Derivative
$(3x+1)^5$$u^5$$3x+1$$5(3x+1)^4 \cdot 3 = 15(3x+1)^4$
$\sin(x^2)$$\sin u$$x^2$$\cos(x^2) \cdot 2x$
$e^{5x}$$e^u$$5x$$5e^{5x}$
$\ln(\cos x)$$\ln u$$\cos x$$\dfrac{1}{\cos x} \cdot (-\sin x) = -\tan x$
$\sqrt{x^2+1}$$\sqrt{u}$$x^2+1$$\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+1}}$

Chaining Multiple Times

For $y = f(g(h(x)))$: $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = f'(g(h(x))) \cdot g'(h(x)) \cdot h'(x)$

📝 Example
$\dfrac{d}{dx}\!\left[e^{\sin(3x)}\right] = e^{\sin(3x)} \cdot \cos(3x) \cdot 3 = 3e^{\sin(3x)}\cos(3x)$

3.2 Implicit Differentiation

When $y$ is defined implicitly by an equation (not solved for $y$), differentiate both sides with respect to $x$ and use the chain rule on any term involving $y$ (appending $\cdot\dfrac{dy}{dx}$), then solve for $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$.

📐 Procedure
  1. Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$.
  2. Every time you differentiate a term in $y$, multiply by $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$.
  3. Collect all $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ terms on one side.
  4. Factor out $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ and solve.
📝 Classic Example — Unit Circle
Given $x^2 + y^2 = 25$, find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$.

Differentiate: $2x + 2y\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0$
Solve: $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{x}{y}$

3.3 Derivatives of Inverse Functions

✅ Inverse Function Derivative Theorem
If $f$ is differentiable and one-to-one with inverse $f^{-1}$, then: $$(f^{-1})'(a) = \frac{1}{f'(f^{-1}(a))}$$ Equivalently: if $f(b) = a$, then $(f^{-1})'(a) = \dfrac{1}{f'(b)}$.
📌 Interpretation
The slope of $f^{-1}$ at point $(a,b)$ equals the reciprocal of the slope of $f$ at point $(b,a)$ — the graphs are reflections across $y=x$.

Key AP exam formulas — arcsin and arctan are the most frequently tested:

$$\frac{d}{dx}[\arcsin(u)] = \frac{u'}{\sqrt{1-u^2}} \qquad \frac{d}{dx}[\arctan(u)] = \frac{u'}{1+u^2}$$

3.5 Higher-Order Derivatives

Higher-order derivatives are derivatives of derivatives:

$$f''(x) = \frac{d}{dx}[f'(x)], \qquad f'''(x) = \frac{d}{dx}[f''(x)], \qquad f^{(n)}(x) = \frac{d^n f}{dx^n}$$
📌 Physical Interpretation
If $s(t)$ is position: $v(t) = s'(t)$ = velocity, $a(t) = s''(t) = v'(t)$ = acceleration, $j(t) = s'''(t)$ = jerk (rate of change of acceleration).

Example: $f(x) = x^5 \Rightarrow f'(x) = 5x^4 \Rightarrow f''(x) = 20x^3 \Rightarrow f^{(5)}(x) = 120 \Rightarrow f^{(6)}(x) = 0$.

Worked Examples

Example 1Chain Rule — Nested Trig

Find $\dfrac{d}{dx}[\cos^3(2x)]$.

1
Outermost: power of 3. Middle: cosine. Inner: $2x$. $$= 3\cos^2(2x) \cdot \frac{d}{dx}[\cos(2x)]$$
2
$\dfrac{d}{dx}[\cos(2x)] = -\sin(2x) \cdot 2$
3
$$= 3\cos^2(2x) \cdot (-\sin(2x)) \cdot 2 = \boxed{-6\cos^2(2x)\sin(2x)}$$
Example 2Chain Rule — Composite Exponential

Differentiate $y = e^{x^2 - 4x}$.

1
Outer: $e^u$, inner: $u = x^2 - 4x$, so $u' = 2x - 4$.
2
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = e^{x^2-4x} \cdot (2x-4) = \boxed{(2x-4)e^{x^2-4x}}$
Example 3Implicit Differentiation — Ellipse

Find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ for $x^3 + y^3 = 6xy$.

1
Differentiate both sides w.r.t. $x$: $$3x^2 + 3y^2\frac{dy}{dx} = 6y + 6x\frac{dy}{dx}$$
2
Gather $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ terms: $$3y^2\frac{dy}{dx} - 6x\frac{dy}{dx} = 6y - 3x^2$$
3
Factor and solve: $$\frac{dy}{dx}(3y^2 - 6x) = 6y - 3x^2 \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = \boxed{\frac{2y - x^2}{y^2 - 2x}}$$
Example 4Implicit — Tangent Line on Circle

Find the slope of the tangent to $x^2 + y^2 = 13$ at the point $(2, 3)$.

1
Differentiate: $2x + 2y\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0 \Rightarrow \dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{x}{y}$
2
At $(2,3)$: $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{2}{3}$. Verify $(2,3)$ on circle: $4+9=13$ ✓. Slope $= \boxed{-\dfrac{2}{3}}$.
Example 5Inverse Function Derivative

Let $f(x) = x^3 + 2x + 1$. Find $(f^{-1})'(4)$.

1
We need $f(b) = 4$. Test $b=1$: $1+2+1=4$ ✓. So $f^{-1}(4) = 1$.
2
$f'(x) = 3x^2 + 2$. At $b=1$: $f'(1) = 3+2 = 5$.
3
$(f^{-1})'(4) = \dfrac{1}{f'(1)} = \boxed{\dfrac{1}{5}}$
Example 6Inverse Trig + Chain Rule

Find $\dfrac{d}{dx}[\arctan(3x^2)]$.

1
Use $\dfrac{d}{dx}[\arctan(u)] = \dfrac{u'}{1+u^2}$ with $u = 3x^2$, $u' = 6x$.
2
$$= \frac{6x}{1+(3x^2)^2} = \boxed{\frac{6x}{1+9x^4}}$$
Example 7Higher-Order Derivatives

If $y = \sin x$, find $y''$ and $y^{(4)}$.

1
$y = \sin x \Rightarrow y' = \cos x \Rightarrow y'' = -\sin x$
2
$y''' = -\cos x \Rightarrow y^{(4)} = \sin x$. The cycle repeats every 4 derivatives: $\boxed{y^{(4)} = \sin x}$.
